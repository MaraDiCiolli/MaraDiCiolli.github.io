---

*Thinking in Systems* by Donella Meadows was the first book that made me realize feedback loops aren't just a software concept — they're everywhere, from economics to ecology. Once you see them, you can't unsee them.

*How Buildings Learn* by Stewart Brand changed how I think about software architecture. Brand argues that the best buildings are the ones designed to be changed. The same is true for codebases. Design for adaptation, not permanence.

*Seeing Like a State* by James C. Scott is a masterclass in understanding why top-down systems fail. Every time I see an over-engineered abstraction layer, I think about Scott's critique of "high modernism" — the belief that legibility from above is more important than functionality on the ground.
